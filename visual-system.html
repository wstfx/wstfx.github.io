Zebang Wu / Visual language / 2026

A little wild.
Carefully drawn.

An orange fox, a clear line, and room for curiosity. A shared language for character, illustration, typography, and motion.

01 / One character, two voices

Filled with energy.
Light on its feet.

A tapered muzzle, long limbs, pointed ears, and a broad flowing tail define the silhouette. Curves carry the movement; a few colored planes give it depth.

Use the filled fox as a focal point. Use its contour counterpart in margins, transitions, and supporting decoration. Keep both silhouettes clear and give the tail room to move.

02 / A small vocabulary of gestures

Presence through posture.

Alert, attentive, curious, in motion, and turning back. Each pose keeps the same anatomy, color rhythm, and line character.

05 / Illustration and motion

Make the idea
visible.

Start with the headline.

Listening begins with a person. A question meets resistance. A useful thing reaches someone, and a response comes back. Every scene expresses the chapter’s central action.

Let motion explain.

A token follows an idea, a boundary responds, and a feedback path returns. Character gestures stay smaller than the scene’s main action.

Leave room to read.

Use one principal motion at a time. Keep labels still, respect reduced-motion preferences, and pause scenes when they are out of view.
