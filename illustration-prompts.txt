Create recognizable, project-connected editorial scenes for the homepage. These are concepts for image generation and art direction, not replacements for authentic project screenshots or records.

00 · Beginning / Follow a question. See where it leads.

Create one finished editorial illustration for a personal research-and-design portfolio, one single frame, near-square composition suitable for a 600 × 560 canvas. On a warm paper background (#F7F5ED), an elegant orange fox stands beside a low, simply drawn sage workbench and touches the curled edge of a broad cream sheet with one front paw. The sheet gently unrolls from the workbench into a short, shallow, winding path in the foreground; one very small model car sits at the far end. Make the fox's focused gaze and the sheet opening into an unexpected route the main story: curiosity leads into making and testing. The fox has a slender torso, long tapered legs, pointed ears and muzzle, a small dark nose, a calm almond eye, and a large flowing brush tail with a cream tip. Use the attached approved fox reference consistently. Build the fox from a few precise curved geometric color planes in warm orange #F47B20, burnt orange #D84B12, apricot #FFAF6A, and cream #FFF0DA. Supporting shapes are forest green #193D30 and sage #B7C4A5. Keep the drawing modern, succinct, vivid, and elegant, with clean edges, a shallow three-quarter view, and generous open paper around the action. This is conceptual editorial art, not a product screenshot. No text, lettering, question-mark symbols, interface, logos, decorative orb diagrams, collage, grid, presentation board, textured fur, giant eyes, photorealism, or checkerboard. Show only this one scene, not multiple versions.

The student leans forward slightly with relaxed open posture; the fox looks toward the student, ears gently alert, with no exaggerated smile. Between them is a small low side table carrying one open notebook with blank cream pages. The notebook is peripheral; their attentive facing posture and the open space between them carry the story. A few tiny dark measurement pins occupy an already explored part of the surface; the far side is simpler, paler, and less described. At the boundary between these regions, one distinct orange measurement pin sits beneath a freestanding circular magnifying lens. The fox gently touches the base of this pin with a front paw while looking through the lens.
