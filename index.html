01

NOTICE / THE HUMAN QUESTION

Start with
someone.

Before asking what a system can do, ask whose experience needs a closer look.

In my research with Deaf and Hard-of-Hearing students, that means listening: designing interviews, working through accounts of well-being, and looking for themes without flattening individual experiences.

RESEARCH · MAY 2024–PRESENTListening to student well-being ↗Interview design, fieldwork coordination & thematic analysis, under faculty supervision.

At a very different scale, FeasyPaste began with a familiar friction: moving an AI response into Feishu without losing its equations or formatting.

↘

But a useful question needs
more than intuition.

02

TEST / THE RESEARCH QUESTION

Give curiosity
something
to push against.

What would let me tell whether an idea actually works?

In active learning, I explored how a model chooses what to learn next. The limits of an initial neural query strategy led to a revised approach based on error reduction.

RESEARCH · DEC 2024–JUL 2025Choosing what a model learns next ↗From neural query strategies to error-reduction sampling.
An idea→A comparison→A better question

Today, my work on physical-field reconstruction centers on comparing baselines when sensor trajectories change. A convincing picture is only the beginning; the comparison matters.

↘

An experiment also needs
a system you can trust.

03

BUILD / THE SYSTEMS QUESTION

Then, let
the world
push back.

Working systems make abstract constraints very concrete.

At Dongfeng, a driving model had to agree with its simulator: coordinates, camera inputs, vehicle history, inference, and recovery when an evaluation failed.

ENGINEERING · JUL–AUG 2026VLA models in the driving loop ↗Integration, inference optimization & recoverable evaluation.

The same attention to behavior takes a different form in InVolve: keeping lesson preparation, live distribution, and the student view understandable. I design and build the frontend; my collaborator develops the backend.

PRODUCT · USED IN TEACHINGInVolve ↗Frontend, UI & interaction design for the classroom.
↘

And a working system still has to
make sense to someone.

04

RETURN / THE NEXT QUESTION

Bring it back
to people.

The most interesting part is what happens when someone actually uses it.

I taught an introductory AI-agent coding course through InVolve, for people with no programming experience. The interface I had built became part of my own teaching practice.

TEACHING × INTERACTIONFrom building a tool to teaching with it ↗A real classroom context for InVolve.

That interest in making unfamiliar things approachable also runs through my VR learning work at Cornell and current family-care product work.
