Five scenes on the homepage · 3 October 2026

Your FoxHomepage_0 artwork opens the story; four new vector scenes now accompany the chapters. Review the new scenes, source PNGs, and editable SVGs ↗. The briefs below remain available for the next art-direction pass.

01 / One image first

A scene you can understand
before reading its caption.

Start with the listening scene. It tests the fox’s character, human body language, and clarity together. Generate separate alternatives, choose one, and use it as the reference for the rest.

01

Choose one direction

Begin with the Notice scene because attentive body language, fox anatomy, and human proportions are all visible. Generate the main prompt and the three composition variants separately. Choose a silhouette and composition before generating the full set.

02

Lock the fox

Keep the chosen image as a character reference. Generate one isolated filled fox and one contour version for approval. Record its proportions and palette; reuse this reference in every chapter instead of independently reinventing the mascot.

03

Approve the scene

Check whether an unfamiliar viewer can name the action in a few seconds without reading the caption. Select for composition and storytelling first. Small edge defects can be repaired later; a confusing scene should be regenerated.

04

Clean and separate

Clean selected shapes, remove unwanted texture, and manually correct vector paths after tracing when necessary. A generated PNG is a flat image, and converting it to SVG does not automatically produce meaningful editable layers. Separate only the parts needed for motion; reconstruct pixels or vector shapes hidden behind moving parts.

05

Prepare the handoff

Deliver the approved composite preview plus either an SVG with clearly named groups or separate transparent PNG/WebP parts on the same canvas. Include a clean static background, full shapes behind overlaps, intended pivot locations, and a flattened fallback. Verify real alpha transparency rather than a painted checkerboard. Retain the layered editable master separately.

06

Animate the meaning

Animate at most three elements per scene. Use a 6–10 second cycle with a clear resting period, quiet fox breathing or tail movement, and one purposeful action. Keep people and the overall composition stable. Pause off-screen scenes and provide both a motion control and a reduced-motion still.

Your first handoff can be simple.

Send the chosen image and what you like about it. A layered file is welcome, but not a prerequisite. I can assess whether vector tracing or transparent image layers will preserve it better, then organize the parts needed for animation.

The shared visual direction

Use the user-supplied FoxHomepage_0.svg as the authoritative character. Preserve long forest-green legs, a small tapered head, triangular ears, cream chest and broad cream-tipped brush tail. The eye is a small open angular teardrop with a warm peach iris, dark angled pupil and tiny cream catchlights; preserve the original eye geometry rather than a generic almond or closed smiling eye.

Use flowing geometric color planes built from a few decisive arcs and tapered curves, with pointed accents at ears, muzzle, paws, and tail tip. Prefer three to five large planes within the fox to many tiny facets. Use sparse forest-green contour lines for secondary details. No wireframe construction circles in the finished scenes.

One near-square illustration per chapter, composed for a 600 × 560 website canvas. Ask the generator for the closest available landscape or square size, then crop deliberately. Keep the important action within the central 80%, with approximately one third of the picture left open. Use a shallow three-quarter view rather than a deep environment. One action, one recognizable setting, and at most three supporting objects per image.

Generate no text. Headlines and short explanatory labels remain real HTML in Garet and Best Swashed. The scene should embody the subtitle through posture and objects, rather than repeat it as lettering inside an image.

02 / Cleanup, with a clear job for each tool

Clean edges.
Useful layers.

For flat geometric art, my starting recommendation is Vectorizer.AI, followed by grouping and cleanup in a vector editor. If a selected image relies on subtle shaded planes, keep transparent raster layers instead of forcing it into vectors.

ToolWhere it helpsWhat remains to do
Vectorizer.AI ↗Turn the approved flat-color image into editable SVG paths; reduce the palette and inspect curve quality.An SVG is not a character rig. Group head, tail, body, and props deliberately after conversion.
Recraft ↗A browser alternative for converting selected raster artwork to editable SVG.Check that vectorization preserves the chosen silhouette. Shapes still need meaningful animation groups.
Qwen-Image-Layered ↗Try decomposing a composite image into transparent raster layers, particularly when objects overlap.Experimental production route: layer contents are not explicitly controlled by the prompt. A whole fox may remain one layer; output is not vector artwork.
Illustrator ↗If already available: Image Trace, Expand, Ungroup, and Simplify, then organize the moving parts.This final cleanup and grouping is deliberate editing, not automatic understanding of the scene.
Inkscape ↗A free vector editor for tracing, simplifying paths, and organizing named SVG groups.A useful alternative to Illustrator; it still needs judgment about silhouette, overlap, and pivots.

Sources: each tool name links to its official tool or documentation. Vectorizer.AI export plans · Qwen layer behavior and limitations · Recraft vector editor. Reviewed 2 October 2026; no artwork has been uploaded to these services.

00 · Beginning

Follow a question. See where it leads.

A workbench opens into a path

Curiosity is an invitation to investigate something tangible. The fox discovers a rolled sheet that becomes a small working landscape, foreshadowing research, engineering, and learning without displaying a catalogue of projects.

What the viewer sees

  • An elegant fox investigating a curled sheet
  • A low workbench
  • A small model car on the path that unfurls from the sheet

Grounded in the work

This personal website ↗
The opening introduces the shared habit of following questions across different kinds of work.

VLA models in the driving loop ↗
One tiny model car at the far end of the unfolding path foreshadows a concrete engineering chapter.

Three compositions to explore
  1. Unrolling discovery — Keep the fox in side profile on the left; place the rolled paper near its paw and allow the path to sweep toward the lower right.
  2. A peek around the bend — The fox lowers its head around the upright paper curl; the tiny car becomes visible just beyond the curve. Preserve a large open upper half.
  3. The first step — The sheet is already partly unfurled. The fox takes one tentative step onto it while its tail balances behind; the workbench remains a single minimal supporting shape.

Movement after approval

  • sheet curl complete shape — A small 3–5 degree roll and settle around its contact point; do not reveal new scene content during the loop.
  • fox head and neck — A small curious tilt toward the opening, followed by a long resting pause.
  • fox tail full root — A slow shallow sweep, with the root hidden behind the body.

Keep out of the image

  • A magical portal or floating question mark replacing the concrete workbench
  • Many miniature project symbols competing with the first action
  • A scene dependent on text to explain the path

01 · Notice

Start with someone.

Make room for a conversation

The scene should communicate attentive listening before it communicates research. An adult student and a fox meet at eye level around a modest conversation setting; the space between them is open and welcoming.

What the viewer sees

  • One adult student seated comfortably
  • A fox sitting attentively beside an empty chair
  • One open notebook on a small side table

Grounded in the work

Listening to student well-being ↗
Interview design and attention to individual accounts are the chapter's concrete starting point. The illustration depicts the act of listening, not a verified participant or a particular communication method.

Three compositions to explore
  1. At eye level — Use the facing side-profile composition in the main prompt; this makes mutual attention immediately legible.
  2. An open place — Move the empty chair slightly into the foreground and have the fox sit beside it, creating a visual invitation to join the conversation.
  3. A shared pause — Use a closer crop of the seated adult and fox, with the notebook only partly visible at the lower edge; retain generous negative space between their faces.

Movement after approval

  • fox head and neck — A gentle 2 degree attentive tilt once per cycle, not constant nodding.
  • fox tail full root — A small resting tail-tip lift and settle.
  • fox body — Subtle breathing with less than 1% scale change around the lower torso.

Keep out of the image

  • A childlike student or a fox delivering a lecture
  • Invented signs of disability or a claim about actual participants' appearance
  • Bouncing speech bubbles or sound-based imagery standing in for listening

02 · Test

Give curiosity something to push against.

Choose the next place to measure

Make investigation tangible: the fox chooses a measurement point on a partly understood surface. Viewers should see an experiment and a decision, rather than an abstract network. The miniature landscape is a metaphor for sampling a continuous field, not a claim that a physical experiment took place.

What the viewer sees

  • A shallow tabletop landscape with a few smooth contour bands
  • Several small measurement pins
  • A fox examining a new location through a simple freestanding lens

Grounded in the work

Choosing what a model learns next ↗
Selecting an informative next sample provides a clear physical action for the fox.

Reconstruction beyond one sensor path ↗
A sparse measured path across a field connects to the ongoing interest in sensing and comparison without inventing a research result.

Three compositions to explore
  1. The next measurement — Use the main prompt with the fox at the left and the selected point near the center; the lens creates one strong circular focal shape.
  2. Look closer — Use a lower side view: the fox's muzzle approaches the lens while the orange pin remains visible through it. Keep the terrain shallow rather than mountainous.
  3. A deliberate choice — Show the fox's paw just short of the orange pin, emphasizing the moment before committing to the next measurement; simplify the lens to a thin forest contour.

Movement after approval

  • selected pin — Rise 4 pixels, settle into place, and rest; add one restrained outline pulse only if it remains readable.
  • fox head and neck — Lean a few pixels toward the lens, synchronized with the measurement and then pause.
  • fox tail full root — A slow counterbalancing sweep behind the fox.

Keep out of the image

  • Presenting the sampled surface as real measured project data
  • A winner-versus-loser comparison implying unverified performance
  • Floating equations, laboratory spectacle, or a generic constellation of nodes

03 · Build

Then, let the world push back.

The road is the test

A model car encounters the constraints of a small test road. The fox examines the point where the planned path meets a bend and an obstacle, making integration, feedback, and adjustment concrete.

What the viewer sees

  • A small unbranded model car
  • A compact curved road in a shallow test tray
  • One traffic cone and a fox inspecting the turn

Grounded in the work

VLA models in the driving loop ↗
The driving-simulator integration work gives this chapter a readily identifiable system and environment. The miniature track is a conceptual depiction of simulation, not physical vehicle testing.

Three compositions to explore
  1. At the bend — Keep the fox left and road right, with the car just before the cone; make the turn and inspection the visual center.
  2. Check the alignment — Use a lower viewpoint that emphasizes the fox inspecting the car's heading relative to the road. The planned line remains one quiet curve.
  3. Run, observe, adjust — Place the fox behind the shallow tray and the car in the near foreground, preserving its visible path around the one cone; do not add new tools or screens.

Movement after approval

  • car complete shape — Travel a short distance toward the bend, pause, and continue gently around it; reset only when hidden or between chapter visits.
  • planned path — Reveal one short route segment just ahead of the car, then leave it still.
  • fox head and neck — Track the car with a small controlled tilt, then settle.

Keep out of the image

  • Implying the internship involved building or testing a physical toy car
  • A car racing, drifting, crashing, or continuously circling the reader's peripheral vision
  • A large cityscape that overwhelms the simple test

04 · Return

Bring it back to people.

A tool becomes a learning moment

Show the transition from building an interface to teaching with it: adult beginners gather around a laptop, one tries something, another asks a question, and the fox turns toward that person. The human exchange is the point of the picture.

What the viewer sees

  • Two adult learners at one modest worktable
  • One open laptop viewed mainly from behind
  • A fox turning toward the learner who is asking a question

Grounded in the work

A first encounter with AI coding ↗
The author taught people with no prior coding experience using InVolve; this supplies a real context for learning by doing.

InVolve ↗
The author designs the frontend and interactions, then uses the tool in teaching. The scene conveys that relationship without inventing the product's actual interface.

Three compositions to explore
  1. The next question — Use the main prompt. Place the questioning learner at the right, keyboard learner at the center, and fox at the left turning toward the question.
  2. Learning side by side — Use a closer view around the table with the fox between the two adult figures in the composition, while keeping the laptop's back visible and the gaze exchange unobstructed.
  3. An invitation to try — One learner has just moved their hands toward the keyboard while the other watches; the fox gives them space and looks on attentively. Keep the action modest, with no invented success signal.

Movement after approval

  • fox head and neck — Turn a few degrees toward the questioning learner, hold, and return slowly.
  • lesson sheet complete shape — A barely lifted corner settles once, providing a quiet sign of use without animating a fictional interface.
  • fox tail full root — One slow relaxed sweep under or beside the platform; keep it clear of the people.

Keep out of the image

  • An imitation of InVolve UI, invented classroom responses, or a claimed student outcome
  • A one-way fox lecture in front of passive children
  • A decorative house, garden, robot, or floating network unrelated to the teaching moment

Pick for the still.
Animate for the meaning.

Without seeing the heading, can a viewer describe the action as exploring, listening, testing, operating, or teaching?

  • The fox reads as the same character in every scene, including muzzle, legs, and tail.
  • The key object connects to a named project without needing a caption to rescue an unrelated image.
  • The illustration adds context to the headline and leaves the website typography readable.
  • No illustrated screen could be mistaken for a capture of InVolve or another real product.
  • The still composition works before animation; motion clarifies the action instead of supplying missing meaning.
  • Each moving part can be isolated and moved without exposing a hole or cutting across another object.
